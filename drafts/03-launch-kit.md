# Launch kit: "I Was Checking His Blood for a Side Effect. It Was Leukemia."

## What I changed and why (review before publishing)

**Must-fix (professional risk)**

- **Removed "her personality disorder."** Implying a diagnosis of someone you haven't examined is the Goldwater Rule (APA ethics, §7.3), the same issue the checklist flagged on the Scott Jennings Note. It's replaced with: *"I have not examined her, and I don't diagnose people from documentaries."* The restraint now reads as a trust signal.
- **De-identified the patient** (your choice): "twenty-one" → "early twenties"; the medical-director title and the chemo drug are cut; a footer line says details were changed. I'm sorry about the "signed more forms" joke. It was the most linkable detail. Restore it if you judge it safe.

**Accuracy (checked against primary sources)**

- **WBC "18.4, when it should have been less than half that"** → *"when normal tops out around 11."* Half of 18.4 is 9.2, which is inside the normal range.
- **Watching the white cells is framed as your habit**, not universal practice. The quetiapine label calls for frequent CBCs only with prior low counts, and the APA 2020 guideline calls for a baseline CBC. A psychiatrist reader would have caught the original. It also makes your caution the reason the cancer was found.
- **"Lymphoblastic" throughout** (NCI's term). "Normal neutrophils often drop first" → *"normal blood counts can fall."* The cited paper was a single case report that calls this rare.
- **Three bad citations replaced.** PMID 16930797 is Cowan & Oakley, not Lander & Bastiampillai; it's swapped for AMSP surveillance data. The cytarabine paper had the wrong title and was a negative trial, so it's dropped. The fingerstick claims are now backed by WHO and Bond & Richards-Kortum.
- **Walgreens:** "about forty stores, nearly all in Arizona" (40 in AZ plus 1 in Palo Alto). Cut "ten-billion-dollar darling," because the deal was signed in 2010 and the valuation came in 2014. Cut "terrified," which stated motive as fact.
- **Safeway** didn't walk away after due diligence. It spent about $350M on clinics, and the testing never launched.
- **Cut "890,000 tests, 1-in-10 error rate."** The paper gives no source for it, and the 1-in-10 was Walgreens' voided-report rate from its lawsuit, not a measured error rate. **Cut the plural "imaginary heart attacks,"** since there is one documented ER case.
- **Cut the investors-vs-patients verdict line.** Sunny Balwani *was* convicted on the patient counts, so the line would have invited a correction. "Billionaire venture capitalists" was also wrong: the investors were a hedge fund and family offices.
- **Removed the Amanda Seyfried detail.** It's a spoiler the filmmakers asked audiences to keep.

**Register and structure**

- "Garbage," "corporate cowardice," "made my blood boil," and "the loophole we refused to close" became structural framing built on your word, *misallocation*: "Each layer assumed another layer had done the checking." I also added *"Reasonable people disagree about whether the FDA is the right referee"* so readers of every politics stay with you.
- **Named diagnostic overshadowing.** It's the being-believed thread, and it's your authority lane.
- **Added "What to do with a number"** plus the Corner Kit. That's the practical turn the pivot formula needs.
- **Cut about 300 words.** "Do I have cancer?" appeared three times, so the middle use is gone. The "(PMC)" and "(Salon)" tags are now inline links, which read better on a phone.

## Before you hit publish (about 10 minutes)

1. **Make the Corner Kit link work.** In Drive, open `The_Corner_Kit_-_Lab_Tests_-_one-page_PDF.pdf` → Share → General access → **Anyone with the link, Viewer**. The essay already links to that file. Until you flip it, readers will hit a permission wall.
2. **Paste into Substack.** Copy everything from the essay doc's first paragraph down. Put the title and the italic subtitle into Substack's **Title** and **Subtitle** fields, not the body.
3. **SEO settings** (Settings → SEO options in the post editor):
   - Search title: *Nathan Fielder's Holmes Doc, Seen by a Psychiatrist*
   - Search description: *A routine lab caught a young man's leukemia. A psychiatrist on Theranos, lab-developed tests, and what to do with a surprising number.*
   - People are searching the film right now. The email title tells your story; the search title is there to catch the film's search traffic.
4. **Social preview image.** Don't use a still from the film, because A24 owns those. Use your own photo or a plain text card with the line *"In an exam room, a blood test is a load-bearing wall."*
5. **Add a Subscribe button** after the last line of the essay, above the footer.
6. **Read it once on your phone** in Substack's preview. Most of your readers will.

**If news breaks before you publish:** Holmes's commutation request was still pending as of the last reporting. If she's released, change "Holmes is in federal prison" to match.
