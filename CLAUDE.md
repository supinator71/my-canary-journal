## Publication identity

- **Name:** The Clinical Lens, by Supriya Kang, MD
- **Author:** board-certified psychiatrist, 20+ years, built her career on the
  hardest cases — incarcerated patients, severely/chronically ill, people the
  system had written off. Currently runs a small virtual (telepsychiatry) practice.
- **Core clinical thesis** (from the About page, her strongest material):
  outcomes were almost never about diagnosis — they were about whether the
  patient had a bed, a locked door, food, someone who believed them. "Health is
  housing. Health is safety. Health is being believed." That line is the load-bearing
  idea under everything else; worth pulling forward/reusing rather than burying
  mid-page.
- **Stated brand promise:** "Evidence first, always."
- **Self-description:** reserved/quiet in person; the newsletter is explicitly
  framed as the place she stops being quiet. "Quiet has become a luxury I can't afford."
- Free, no paywall, by design ("not here to gate this, I'm here to reach people").
- Standard disclaimer: views are hers, not an employer's; commentary/education,
  not medical advice; points to 988 for readers in crisis.

## Content structure / voice (essays)

Recurring formula, executed consistently: **personal/clinical vignette → nervous-
system or clinical mechanism → political/current-events point.** Deer at the
window, a cat and a screen door, a patient from residency, her own habituation —
always grounded in a concrete image before the argument arrives.

**Title pattern risk:** "[Field] of [Concept]" is used 4/16 times — "The Politics
of Fear," "The Biology of the Freeze," "The Biology of Scale," "The Geopolitics
of D.A.R.V.O." Good device, starting to feel like a tic on repeat. The strongest
titles break the pattern and go narrative: "Coming Down the Hill," "The Man Who
Won the Lottery," "The Floor, the Screen Door, and What Comes Next," "They Fired
Scott Pelley, and I Wasn't Surprised. That Is the Problem."

**Essays vs. Notes voice gap:** essays earn their claims (vignette → mechanism →
point); Notes are much rawer/more partisan ("beholden to our oligarchs," "same
media suppression playbook as the Nazis," "it's time to stop that bullshit").
Legitimate as a deliberate two-register choice, but it's in tension with the
"evidence first, always" brand promise — worth an explicit decision rather than
letting it drift.

**Notes-as-workshop pattern:** Notes sometimes preview an essay days ahead
("A follow-up to last week. The biology of the freeze...") — a good instinct,
worth doing on purpose for every essay if not already.
